Conversational Query Layer for Rice Breeding Trial Data

The problem

Salt-tolerance breeding programs generate large volumes of trial data: variety performance across seasons, salinity thresholds, SES screening scores, and marker information such as the Saltol QTL. This data typically lives in spreadsheets, PDFs, and scattered publications. Answering a simple question, for example which aman-season varieties held yield above 4 t/ha under salinity, or how Pokkali compares with FL478, requires manual digging through those files each time.

The solution

A conversational query layer: researchers ask plain-language questions and get answers with the actual numbers, drawn from the underlying data. No spreadsheet work, no manual cross-referencing.

Why a curated dataset, not just an AI chatbot

A general AI chatbot predicts plausible-sounding text. Asked for a variety's saline yield or release year, it can invent a confident, wrong number — and the researcher has no way to know. In breeding, a wrong number means wrong crosses and wasted seasons.

This demo inverts that: the language model never answers from its own knowledge. It only composes from rows retrieved out of published trial tables, cites the source behind every number, and says plainly when the dataset does not cover a question. Traceability is the design goal — an assistant a breeder can check, not just chat with.

How it works

  1. Compiled dataset. 71 rice varieties — salt-tolerant BRRI and BINA releases, landrace donors such as Pokkali and Nona Bokra, breeding lines such as FL478, standard checks such as IR29, plus major mega-varieties (Swarna, Samba Mahsuri, IR36), basmatis (Pusa Basmati 1121 and others) and hybrids — with yields, salinity thresholds, SES scores, and Saltol marker information. Every value is taken from published IRRI, BRRI, and BINA trial tables; each row cites its source. Blank cells mean the value was not found in the consulted sources.
  2. Deterministic retrieval. A question is parsed for variety names, yield thresholds, seasons, tolerance classes, and marker terms, and the matching rows are pulled from the dataset. No guessing.
  3. Grounded answering. A language model (accessed through a self-hosted AI gateway, no third-party data sharing) composes the answer strictly from the retrieved rows. It is instructed to cite the numbers, to say when values come from different studies and are not strictly comparable, and to state plainly when the dataset does not cover a question rather than invent an answer.

Current status

A working demo is live: a simple chat interface over the compiled dataset, verified with ranking, comparison, threshold, marker, and out-of-scope questions — breeding-chat-demo.vercel.app. It is a demonstration on published data, built by a final-year B.Sc. Biotechnology student, and claims no affiliation with IRRI, BRRI, BINA, or BRAC.

The ask

To make this useful for BRAC's breeding programs, the layer needs to run on real program data rather than published tables. What would help:

  • The typical format of your marker and trial data (file types, columns, approximate size), so the demo can be rebuilt around your actual workflow.
